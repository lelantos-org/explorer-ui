Explorer — what carries over AND WHAT COMES BACK

Seven checks. Four of the wallet's improvements apply here, one does not, and — worth saying plainly — two go the other way: the explorer already solves problems the wallet still has, and solves them better.

01 Two different percentages, one word → APPLY TO EXPLORER
The explorer's Return column is indexGrowth(indexRay) — total growth since the venue was bound, not annualised. The wallet's Pool pays is apy — annualised over a measured window. Both render as a percentage, about the same venue, in two products a user moves between. Nothing marks them as incomparable. Do: Rename and qualify in place. Return becomes “Since bound”; Pool pays keeps “/ yr · 30d”. Neither ever appears as a bare percentage.
02 Nine cards, all shouting equally → APPLY TO EXPLORER
Supported assets, chain flows, escrow, inflow/outflow, transactions by kind, withdrawal anonymity, latest transactions, pool notes — stacked at one weight on a single page. This is exactly the bloat the wallet's Home had before it was cut back to five things. Do: Lead with the two things only this explorer has — flows and withdrawal anonymity. Registry, escrow and pool notes become reference, further down or behind a tab.
03 Protocol words on a public page → APPLY TO EXPLORER
“Pool notes”, “leaves”, “relayer notes”, “escrowed by chain”, “commitments”. The wallet moved from deposit/withdraw to Shield/Unshield for exactly this reason. The explorer's audience is more technical, but not uniformly so — the anonymity card in particular is the thing a non-expert most needs to read. Do: Keep the protocol term as the subtitle, lead with the plain one: “Notes in the tree”, “Held in the pool”, “Withdrawal cover”.
04 Everything inline, nothing on tap → APPLY TO EXPLORER
Each card carries its full caption — lead, basis and gaps — permanently, in small grey type. The wallet moved the same class of qualification into a row detail so the surface could breathe. Do: Keep the one-line lead visible. Move basis and gaps behind the row or card, where there is room to say them in full sentences.
05 The three-state data shape ← ADOPT FROM EXPLORER
useAsync.ts returns {data, loading, error} so that, in its own words, no caller has to guess “whether an empty result means still loading, nothing there, or the backend is down — the three used to be indistinguishable wherever a failure was swallowed into [].” Do: The wallet still does the swallow: its forms read data?.balances ?? [], which is wallet finding #5. Port this shape across — the explorer already proved it.
06 Per-card honest captions ← ADOPT FROM EXPLORER
meta.ts gives every card a lead, a basis and a gaps line — “USD at spot · partial”, “deposits − withdrawals”, “3 unpriced assets excluded”. The discipline of naming what a figure rests on, per card, is stronger than anything in the wallet. Do: Adopt the pattern in the wallet's portfolio and fee panels rather than inventing new phrasing there.
07 Review steps, fee assets, the link vault NOT APPLICABLE
The wallet's three highest-severity findings were all about irreversible actions and stored secrets. The explorer is read-only, holds no keys and signs nothing. Do: Nothing to carry over. Noted so the audit is complete rather than selective.

05 — the flow reverses The wallet's fifth-worst finding was that a syncing wallet is indistinguishable from an empty one. The explorer fixed that class of bug at the data layer and wrote down why.
